## Understanding Transitions and the Logic They Signal

On the Reading and Writing section of the Digital SAT, transition questions test your ability to recognize the logical relationship between two sentences or clauses. Transition words do not merely connect ideas; they signal the direction of an argument. They tell the reader whether an idea is continuing in the same direction, shifting to an opposing point, or serving as a cause, effect, or example. To choose the correct transition—and to understand passages that feature sophisticated vocabulary—you must analyze the precise relationship between statements.

The Digital SAT features two adaptive modules for Reading and Writing, each containing 27 questions with a 32-minute time limit. All questions use short passages accompanied by a single question, with scores ranging from 200 to 800 for the section. Vocabulary is tested directly through Words in Context questions that ask, "Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?" However, strong vocabulary knowledge is equally essential for transition questions, where understanding subtle shifts in tone and argument is critical.

### How Logic Signals Work

Transitions fall into three main logical categories:

1. **Continuance or Addition**: Words like *furthermore*, *in addition*, and *indeed* indicate that the second idea expands upon or strengthens the first. If an author describes a student who accepts a task with [alacrity](/words/alacrity), and then notes that the student completed it ahead of schedule, the second sentence supports the first.

2. **Contrast or Concession**: Words like *however*, *nevertheless*, and *conversely* signal a turn in logic. For instance, an administration might initially appear [amenable](/words/amenable) to student proposals; *however*, if they later [rescind](/words/rescind) those policy changes, a clear contrast exists between their initial openness and their final action.

3. **Cause, Effect, or Conclusion**: Words like *therefore*, *consequently*, and *thus* show a result. If a researcher conducts only a [cursory](/words/cursory) review of the data, *consequently*, the conclusions drawn may exhibit [aberrant](/words/aberrant) results that deviate from established scientific norms.

When solving transition questions, cover the answer choices first. Read the passage carefully, identify the relationship between the two sentences, and state the logic in your own words before selecting an option.

### Worked SAT Examples

#### Example 1
Many historical scholars believed that the ancient monastic order maintained a strictly [ascetic](/words/ascetic) lifestyle, denying themselves all basic material comforts. Recent archaeological discoveries at the site revealed luxury goods and imported tableware. ______, historians are re-evaluating their assumptions about the daily practices of the monks.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

A) Furthermore,
B) Consequently,
C) For instance,
D) Nevertheless,

**Answer:** B  
**Explanation:** The first two sentences set up a premise and new evidence that contradicts it. The third sentence states the result of this new evidence: historians are changing their views. The correct logical relationship is cause-and-effect. *Consequently* correctly signals that the re-evaluation is a direct result of the archaeological findings.

#### Example 2
When the committee proposed a complete overhaul of the school curriculum, several senior faculty members remained [obstinate](/words/obstinate), refusing to modify their traditional teaching methods. The student body, by contrast, displayed total [apathy](/words/apathy), showing no interest in whether the changes were implemented.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

A) In contrast,
B) Similarly,
C) In fact,
D) Ultimately,

**Answer:** A  
**Explanation:** The passage contrasts two different groups: the faculty members who actively resisted the changes and the students who showed complete indifference. The transition must signal a contrast between active stubbornness and passive disinterest. *In contrast* (or *by contrast* as embedded in the context) accurately reflects this shift.

#### Example 3
The board expected the new chief executive to [acquiesce](/words/acquiesce) to their established corporate policies without protest. ______, she executed an [audacious](/words/audacious) restructuring plan during her first week, catching the directors completely off guard.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

A) Indeed,
B) Instead,
C) As a result,
D) For example,

**Answer:** B  
**Explanation:** The first sentence describes what the board expected (passive compliance). The second sentence describes what actually happened (a bold, unexpected intervention). Because the outcome was the opposite of the expectation, a transition showing replacement or contrast is required. *Instead* correctly signals this relationship.

### Words to Know for SAT

- **[aberrant](/words/aberrant)**: Departing from an accepted, normal, or standard type or behavior.
- **[acquiesce](/words/acquiesce)**: To accept, comply, or submit passively without open agreement or active protest.
- **[alacrity](/words/alacrity)**: Brisk, eager, and cheerful readiness or promptness in response.
- **[amenable](/words/amenable)**: Open and responsive to suggestion, advice, or persuasive arguments.
- **[apathy](/words/apathy)**: A lack of interest, enthusiasm, or concern.
- **[ascetic](/words/ascetic)**: Characterized by severe self-discipline and abstention from all forms of indulgence.
- **[audacious](/words/audacious)**: Showing a willingness to take surprisingly bold risks.
- **[cursory](/words/cursory)**: Hasty and superficial, performed without thoroughness or detail.
- **[obstinate](/words/obstinate)**: Stubbornly refusing to change one's opinion or chosen course of action.
- **[rescind](/words/rescind)**: To officially cancel, revoke, or repeal a law, agreement, or decision.

### 5-Minute Daily Practice Routine

1. **Minute 1: Vocabulary Review** — Review two high-frequency SAT vocabulary words. Write down their precise definitions and a original sentence for each.
2. **Minutes 2–3: Transition Identification** — Take two short paragraphs from an editorial or quality news source. Highlight every transition word and classify it (Continuance, Contrast, or Cause/Effect).
3. **Minutes 4–5: Sentence Predictor** — Read a sentence containing an underlined blank for a transition. Before reading the choices, predict whether the logic requires an addition, contrast, or result word.
