## The Core Rule of GRE Text Completion

Every Text Completion prompt on the GRE General Test contains a built-in blueprint for its missing word. Test makers do not expect you to guess what fits based on intuition or subjective style. Instead, each sentence features a specific textual clue—often paired with a transition word—that strictly dictates the definition needed in the blank. For Indian test-takers preparing for the Verbal Reasoning measure, achieving a competitive score on the 130–170 scale requires shifting away from reading for general meaning and shifting toward identifying these structural anchors. If you can locate the clue before reading the answer options, the correct choice becomes unmistakable.

### Understanding Sentence Structure and Clues

The shorter format of the GRE General Test, introduced in September 2023, features two Verbal Reasoning sections: the first presents 12 questions in 18 minutes, and the second presents 15 questions in 23 minutes. Because the test is section-adaptive, doing well on the first section routes you to a more demanding second section where hard vocabulary is tested directly in Text Completion (1–3 blanks) and Sentence Equivalence (choose exactly 2 of 6 answers that give the same meaning). Reading Comprehension rounds out the verbal question types.

To manage the tight time limits of Text Completion, you must approach the sentences methodically:

1. **Locate the Pivot or Direction Marker**: Words like *although*, *yet*, *despite*, and *whereas* indicate a contrast. Words like *furthermore*, *because*, *consequently*, and semicolons indicate continuation or reinforcement.
2. **Identify the Target**: What person, idea, or finding is the blank describing?
3. **Extract the Clue**: Highlight the specific clause or phrase that defines the target.
4. **Formulate Your Own Word**: Choose a simple placeholder word before evaluating the options. This prevents you from being seduced by high-register vocabulary that does not match the prompt's logical requirement.

### Worked Examples

#### Example 1: Single-Blank Text Completion

Despite the board’s initial skepticism regarding the revised timeline, the director was surprisingly ________ to their proposed modifications, readily adjusting the project milestones.

**Options:**
- A. impervious
- B. [amenable](/words/amenable)
- C. indifferent
- D. hostile
- E. resigned

**Answer:** B. amenable

**Why:** The contrast marker "Despite" sets up a shift between the board's "initial skepticism" and the director's actual reaction. The definitive clue appears in the trailing participial phrase: "readily adjusting the project milestones." Because the director adjusted the schedule willingly, the blank must describe someone receptive or cooperative. *Amenable* means open and responsive to suggestion or advice. *Impervious* and *hostile* indicate resistance, *indifferent* means uncaring, and *resigned* suggests passive surrender rather than ready adjustment.

#### Example 2: Two-Blank Text Completion

Because independent laboratories were unable to (i)________ the original experimental findings, the scientific community treated the researcher's ambitious claims as distinctly (ii)________.

| Blank (i) | Blank (ii) |
| :--- | :--- |
| A. [corroborate](/words/corroborate) | D. authoritative |
| B. repudiate | E. negligible |
| C. disseminate | F. [dubious](/words/dubious) |

**Answer:** (i) A. corroborate, (ii) F. dubious

**Why:** The sentence begins with the causal marker "Because," linking the outcome of independent testing directly to the community's assessment. If other labs could not verify the results, the claims would naturally be doubted. For Blank (i), *corroborate* means to confirm or give support to a statement or finding. For Blank (ii), an unconfirmed claim is viewed with suspicion, making *dubious* (hesitating or doubting; not to be relied upon) the logical fit. *Repudiate* would invert the logic, and *authoritative* contradicts the failure of replication.

#### Example 3: Three-Blank Text Completion

As unauthorized digital copies began to (i)________ across regional networks, the studio executives realized they could no longer halt the leak; consequently, rather than launching futile copyright lawsuits, they decided to (ii)________ to the distribution and adopt an entirely (iii)________ monetization strategy.

| Blank (i) | Blank (ii) |
| :--- | :--- |
| A. languish | D. object |
| B. [proliferate](/words/proliferate) | E. [acquiesce](/words/acquiesce) |
| C. dwindle | F. demur |

| Blank (iii) |
| :--- |
| G. orthodox |
| H. unprecedented |
| I. reactionary |

**Answer:** (i) B. proliferate, (ii) E. acquiesce, (iii) H. unprecedented

**Why:** 
- For Blank (i), the phrase "could no longer halt the leak" implies the files were spreading rapidly. *Proliferate* means to increase rapidly in number or amount. 
- For Blank (ii), the clause "rather than launching futile copyright lawsuits" signals that the studio stopped fighting. They chose to yield passively, which precisely defines *acquiesce* (to accept or submit passively without open agreement or active protest). *Object* and *demur* describe resisting or disagreeing, which contradicts their abandonment of lawsuits.
- For Blank (iii), the executives changed tactics away from their traditional response (lawsuits) toward something new to handle the situation. *Unprecedented* reflects this shift to a novel, non-traditional approach. *Orthodox* would mean conventional, and *reactionary* does not match a forward-looking change in monetization.

### Words to Know for GRE

Make sure these five words are part of your active vocabulary:

- **Acquiesce**: To accept, comply, or submit passively without open agreement or active protest.
- **Amenable**: Open and responsive to suggestion, advice, or persuasive arguments.
- **Proliferate**: To increase rapidly in number or amount.
- **Corroborate**: To confirm or give support to a statement, theory, or finding.
- **Dubious**: Hesitating or doubting; not to be relied upon.

Notice how these words often operate as exact functional matches for sentence clues involving agreement, verification, growth, and skepticism.

### 5-Minute Daily Practice Routine

Consistency matters more than marathon study sessions when preparing for GRE Verbal Reasoning. Dedicate five focused minutes each day to this exercise:

1. **Select Two Prompts (1 Minute)**: Pick two official Text Completion questions from your study materials.
2. **Isolate the Clues (2 Minutes)**: Do not look at the choices. Read the prompt, underline the structural pivot (such as *although*, *therefore*, or colons), and highlight the exact phrase that defines each blank.
3. **Fill with Your Own Word (1 Minute)**: Write down a plain-English synonym for each blank on paper.
4. **Match and Check (1 Minute)**: Review the answer choices. Select the word that aligns with your simple placeholder. Confirm whether the missed choices were due to vocabulary gaps or structural misreading.
