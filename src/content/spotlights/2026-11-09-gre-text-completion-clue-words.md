## The blank is never a guess

In a GRE Text Completion, the sentence always contains the evidence for its own answer. Strong test-takers read the sentence, predict a word of their own for the blank, and only then look at the options. Weak test-takers read the options first and pick the one that "sounds GRE".

The evidence comes in two parts: a **clue** (what the blank must describe) and a **signal** (whether the blank agrees with the clue or contrasts with it).

## Signals that keep the same direction

Words like *because*, *and*, *thus*, *indeed* and a colon tell you the blank continues the idea. If the clue says the effect "vanished within days", the blank means *short-lived*: [ephemeral](/words/ephemeral).

## Signals that reverse direction

Words like *although*, *but*, *yet*, *despite* and *far from* tell you the blank is the opposite of the clue. "Far from being clear, the lecture was..." needs a word meaning *unclear*, so [lucid](/words/lucid) would be exactly wrong.

## Three worked examples

**1.** Although the critic's argument was widely quoted, it rested on a ____ premise: the assumption that popularity proves quality.

- (A) lucid (B) fallacious (C) ubiquitous (D) ephemeral (E) pragmatic

Clue: "the assumption that popularity proves quality", a classic error in reasoning. Signal: *although* contrasts "widely quoted" with what the premise really was. Predict *mistaken*. **Answer: (B) fallacious.** *Ubiquitous* is the trap: it fits "widely quoted", not the premise.

**2.** The researchers could not ____ their claim until independent labs reproduced the result.

- (A) substantiate (B) obscure (C) abandon (D) ridicule (E) publicize

Clue: "until independent labs reproduced the result". Reproduction is evidence, so the blank means *prove* or *support*. **Answer: (A) substantiate.** *Publicize* is tempting, but you can publicize a claim without any evidence.

**3.** Smartphones were once a luxury; today they are so ____ that a student without one is the exception.

- (A) ephemeral (B) esoteric (C) ubiquitous (D) lucid (E) anachronistic

Clue: "a student without one is the exception". Signal: *once... today* contrasts past rarity with present. Predict *everywhere*. **Answer: (C) ubiquitous.** *Esoteric* means known to only a few, the opposite of the clue.

## This week's words in GRE context

- [Ephemeral](/words/ephemeral) pairs with time clues: "faded", "brief", "within days".
- [Substantiate](/words/substantiate) pairs with evidence clues: "data", "reproduced", "proof".
- [Fallacious](/words/fallacious) pairs with reasoning clues: "assumption", "premise", "error".
- [Lucid](/words/lucid) pairs with clarity clues, and is the classic wrong answer after "far from".
- [Ubiquitous](/words/ubiquitous) pairs with spread clues: "everywhere", "no longer rare".

## A 5-minute daily routine

1. Cover the options of one Text Completion.
2. Underline the clue and circle the signal word.
3. Write your own word for the blank.
4. Uncover the options and pick the closest match to your word.
5. If you missed it, write down which clue you overlooked.
